# Getting help with mixOmics

## Learning the methods

- The website, <https://mixomics.org>, has tutorials and case studies of the methods.
- The vignette guide, <https://guides.mixomics.org/mixomics-guide-vignette/>, walks through the package step by step.
- Each function has a help page: in R, run `library(mixOmics)` and then `?block.splsda`, for example.

## Questions about an analysis

Please ask on the mixOmics discussion forum: <https://mixomics-users.discourse.group>. It is the place for questions about which method to use, how to set its parameters and how to read a result. Search it first: your question may already have an answer.

You can also ask on the Bioconductor support site, <https://support.bioconductor.org>, with the tag [`mixOmics`](https://support.bioconductor.org/tag/mixOmics/).

When you ask, include the code you ran, the output of `sessionInfo()`, and a description of your data (the number of samples and variables, and the number of blocks or studies).

## Bugs and feature requests

If you think you have found a bug, or you would like a new feature, open an issue at <https://github.com/mixOmics-org/mixOmics/issues> and choose the form that fits. The forms explain what to include.

## Security problems

Do not report a security problem in public. Follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

## Your data

mixOmics runs on the computer where you run R. It does not collect your data or send it anywhere. You are responsible for meeting the data-protection law that applies to the data you analyse. These official pages are a starting point:

- European Union and European Economic Area: [the national data-protection authorities](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/our-members_en) (European Data Protection Board)
- United Kingdom: [Information Commissioner's Office](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/)
- Australia: [Privacy Act 1988](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest)
- New Zealand: [Privacy Commissioner](https://www.privacy.org.nz/)
- Canada: [Office of the Privacy Commissioner](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/)
- United States: there is no general federal data-protection law; for California, see the [California Privacy Protection Agency](https://cppa.ca.gov/)
- Japan: [Personal Information Protection Commission](https://www.ppc.go.jp/en/)
- Republic of Korea: [Personal Information Protection Commission](https://pipc.go.kr/eng/index.do)

This list is not complete. Check the law of the place where you work and of the place the data come from.
